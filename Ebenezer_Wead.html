--- title: "Ebenezer Wead (1748–1830): Frontier Patriot and Pioneer" --- ## Origins in Letterkenny Township, Pennsylvania Ebenezer Wead (also recorded as Wade, Waid, and Wood) was born in 1748 in Letterkenny Township, a frontier region then part of Cumberland County, later Franklin County, Pennsylvania. His father, John Wead, was an early settler who held land documented in Book A, Page 191 of the Pennsylvania land records. This original warrant, surveyed in 1765, covered 479¼ acres and referenced prior settlement improvements, likely cabins or clearings made before 1760. John Wead’s 1760 will was probated shortly after, marking him presumed dead or gravely incapacitated. At just 17 years old, Ebenezer traveled to Philadelphia alone in 1765 to assert his family’s claim—an early display of independence and resilience. ![Land Survey – Ebenezer Wead’s 1765 Warrant](../images/Book_A_68_page_191.png) ## 1765 Land Warrant On May 24, 1765, a proprietary survey issued 479¼ acres to Ebenezer Wead in Letterkenny Township, referencing a warrant originally dated August 28, 1765. The warrant notes “an improvement” made by John Wead prior to the survey. This suggests that the land was being reclaimed post-mortem or in trust for the family line. **Key Citation:** > "A Draught of a Tract of Land situate in Letterkenny Township... surveyed to Ebenezer Wead the 28th Day of August 1765. To pursue a warrant dated May 24th 1765." — Book A, p.191, Department of Internal Affairs --- ## Taxation and Residency Ebenezer appears on the 1783 tax list for York County, Pennsylvania, as “Wead, Ebenezar,” head of a household with 11 inhabitants. He is also found in various Franklin County and Mifflin County tax rolls throughout the 1780s. ![1783 Tax List](../images/Ebenezer_Wead_11_inhabitants.jpg) These records document his persistent presence across multiple counties prior to his westward migration. --- ## Revolutionary War Service (Alias: Ebenezer Wood) During the American Revolution, Ebenezer served under the alias **Ebenezer Wood** in **Lee’s Legion**, a cavalry unit under "Light Horse Harry" Lee. He was later awarded a **bounty land warrant (No. 13926)** for his service. Multiple affidavits and depositions—some decades later—corroborate that the soldier known as Wood was in fact Ebenezer Wead. Supporting documentation includes: - Pension applications - Affidavits from fellow veterans - Bounty land confirmation > "Proving 'Ebenezer Wood' (Lee’s Legion) was Ebenezer Wead" — [Ref. Military Service Files] These materials, collated in `Rev War Bounty Land Warrant #13926`, establish a strong evidentiary chain linking his alias to service and identity. --- ## Migration to Ohio and the Dayton Frontier Around 1799, Ebenezer migrated to **Van Buren Township, Montgomery County, Ohio**. By 1804, he was documented in the first court records of the region. He appears in **Pioneer Life in Dayton and Vicinity**, confirming his presence among Dayton’s earliest settlers. Ebenezer owned multiple tracts of land, including those shown on early 19th-century maps. His descendants remained prominent in the area well into the 1800s. ### Wead Road and Legacy One enduring landmark is **Wead Road**, named after Ebenezer and preserved on early maps of Colonel Robert Patterson’s holdings in Dayton. ![Patterson’s Land Holdings Map Including Wead Road](../images/Patterson_Robert_landholdings.jpg) This map was shared by David Schmidt of Wright College and appears in Charlotte Reeve Conover’s *Concerning the Forefathers*. It confirms Ebenezer’s presence and property footprint adjacent to historic Dayton boundaries. --- ## Timeline Summary | Year | Event | |------|-------| | 1748 | Born in Letterkenny Township, PA | | 1760 | Father's will probated; presumed deceased | | 1765 | Claimed 479¼ acres via proprietary warrant at age 17 | | 1783 | Recorded in York County tax list with 11 inhabitants | | 1777–1781 | Served in Lee’s Legion under the alias Ebenezer Wood | | 1799 | Migrated to Van Buren Township, Ohio | | 1804 | Appeared in Dayton's first court records | | 1830 | Died and buried in Beavertown Cemetery, Kettering, OH | --- ## Known Name Variants | Name | Context | |------|---------| | Ebenezer Wead | Legal documents, Ohio records | | Ebenezer Wade | Franklin County land books | | Ebenezer Wood | Revolutionary War service alias | | Ebenezar Wead | York County tax list (1783) | --- ## Burial and Descendants Ebenezer Wead died in 1830 and was interred in Beavertown Cemetery, Kettering, Ohio.
