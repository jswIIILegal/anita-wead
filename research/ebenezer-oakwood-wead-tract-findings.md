# Ebenezer Wead / Oakwood Wead Tract Research Findings

## Purpose

This memo records the evidence assembled for the Dayton–Oakwood side of the Wead land-mapping project. It separates:

1. **Ebenezer Wead's own Ohio residence and farm**, whose exact parcel is not yet established;
2. the later **Oakwood Wead family tract**, documented under John and Robert Wead and their heirs; and
3. the separate **Babbitt Wead Street**, later Lawnview Avenue, north of Oakwood.

The central evidence rule is that a family association is not the same thing as a proved chain of title. The Oakwood tract should not be labeled as Ebenezer's personal farm unless the deeds or probate records establish that connection.

## Findings at a glance

- The exact Library of Congress record has been identified as **Olmsted Associates Job 6720**, cataloged as: *Patterson, John H.; Wade tract, subdivision "Woodside"; Dayton, Ohio, 1919–1921*.
- The archival spelling **Wade** refers to the same property called the **Wead Farm** in contemporary McCann Plat advertising and local Oakwood history.
- Oakwood Historical Society research describes the land chain as **Patterson family → John and Robert Wead → Wead heirs → John H. Patterson**.
- The historic **Wead Road** was renamed **Patterson Road** in **1909** after John H. Patterson petitioned the court.
- **Dorothy Lane** was formerly **O'Neil Road**; it is not the renamed Wead Road.
- A separate early reference to **John H. Patterson Boulevard** concerns another proposed or early road name and does not identify the replacement name for Wead Road.
- The Babbitt neighborhood's **Wead Street**, later **Lawnview Avenue**, is geographically separate and should remain a separate map feature.
- The strongest present interpretation is that the Oakwood tract documents the land legacy of Ebenezer's next generation, but it does **not yet locate Ebenezer's own farm**.

### What the job file shows

The downloaded job file includes correspondence, field notes, road profiles, topographic plans, subdivision concepts, and the **General Plan for Woodside**. Repeated control features include:

- Patterson Road;
- Shafor Boulevard;
- Schantz Avenue;
- Irving Avenue;
- the Cincinnati, Lebanon & Northern Railroad corridor;
- the wooded tract proposed for park use; and
- open land proposed for streets and building lots.

These controls are sufficient to begin georeferencing the plan, but the final historic parcel polygon should still be reconciled with deeds and recorded plats.

## Wade versus Wead

The spelling variation is internal to the historical record:

- Olmsted and related planning documents often use **Wade Farm** or **Wade Tract**.
- McCann Plat newspaper advertisements call the same land a subdivision of the **Wead Farm**.
- David Schmidt's correspondence with the Oakwood Historical Society explicitly linked the McCann advertisements on the former Wead Farm to the Olmsted job file.
- The family surname was pronounced like **Wade**, making phonetic substitution unsurprising.

The map should preserve the wording of each source while normalizing the identity as **Wead family tract (archival spelling: Wade)**.

## Oakwood ownership chain

Harry G. Ebeling's Oakwood Historical Society essay, *Northeast Oakwood: A Study in Complexity*, gives the following synthesis:

1. Colonel Robert Patterson held the larger federal-patent acreage.
2. His son Francis Patterson sold **71 acres to John Wead** and **80 acres to Robert Wead**.
3. Robert later sold **15 acres to John Wead**.
4. John thereby controlled land described as west of the railroad, east of present Shafor Boulevard, and extending south from Irving Avenue to Wead Road.
5. John Wead's 1893 will left the remaining railroad/Irving Avenue property for the benefit of his daughter Nancy Davis.
6. The Davis heirs sold smaller portions that became the Ohmer and McCann developments.
7. A remaining **53.74 acres** was sold in a 1919 partition process to John H. Patterson.
8. Patterson's children later sold the property to Grant Nichols, who platted **Far Hills Estates** in 1928.

This is a strong local-history reconstruction, but the underlying deed book, page, and probate references still need to be entered into the project's primary-source table.

## Road-name resolution

### Wead Road became Patterson Road

The road-name issue is now substantially resolved. Ebeling's *The Origins of Oakwood's Street Names* states that John H. Patterson petitioned the court to rename **Wead Road**, named for the original owner of the section-line road, as **Patterson Road**, the access to his Far Hills estate.

The companion Oakwood essay dates the change to **1909**.

The family archive also contains a county road image from about 1901 that still labels **Wead Road** at its connection with **Lebanon Pike**. Oakwood historical sources identify Lebanon Pike as present-day **Far Hills Avenue**. This gives a strong historical-to-modern control point at the present Patterson Road / Far Hills Avenue corridor.

### Dorothy Lane is a different renaming

The same street-name essay states that Patterson had **O'Neil Road** renamed **Dorothy Lane** for his daughter. Dorothy Lane therefore should not be used as an alternative name for Wead Road.

### Patterson Boulevard is also separate

The essay mentions that early descriptions near Ridgeway Road and Oakwood Avenue used **John H. Patterson Boulevard**, a name apparently rejected or discontinued. This is a separate naming episode and does not change the conclusion that Wead Road became Patterson Road.

## Separate Babbitt Wead Street

Published neighborhood history identifies a street in the former Babbitt settlement as **Wead Street**, later **Lawnview Avenue**. Babbitt lies north of Irving Avenue in the present University of Dayton student-neighborhood area.

The Oakwood Wead Road, by contrast:

- formed the southern boundary of the Oakwood family tract;
- intersected Lebanon Pike / Far Hills Avenue;
- was renamed Patterson Road in 1909; and
- appears in the Oakwood and Olmsted tract materials.

The two roads should therefore remain separate. The original Babbitt plat or a Dayton street-renaming record is still needed as the primary citation for Wead Street → Lawnview Avenue.

## David Schmidt source notes

The correspondence establishes that David Alan Schmidt:

- supplied the Library of Congress and Olmsted links;
- identified county documents showing portions of J. S. Wade's landholding around 1872 and Wade Road around 1901;
- identified the McCann Plat advertising as being on the former Wead Farm;
- traced the presentation map to Charlotte Reeve Conover's *Concerning the Forefathers*; and
- used a Wright State University email address before directing future correspondence to the Oakwood Historical Society.

The site should refer to **Wright State University**, not "Wright College," and should not assign Schmidt a specific university job title unless a source establishes it.

## Mapping implications

The Dayton-side map should contain three separate evidence objects:

### A. Ebenezer's own settlement area

- Date range: about 1799–1830
- Status: **unresolved exact parcel**
- Anchors: Van Buren Township, Beavertown Cemetery, early court and tax records
- Needed: Ebenezer's deed chain, tax duplicates, probate land description, and neighboring owners

### B. Oakwood Wead family tract

- Principal documented owners: John and Robert Wead and later heirs
- Status: **candidate tract supported by local-history synthesis and historic plans**
- Descriptive bounds: railroad, Shafor Boulevard, Irving Avenue, and historic Wead Road / current Patterson Road
- Later subdivisions: Ohmer, McCann, Woodside proposal, and Far Hills Estates
- Needed: deed-book citations and a georeferenced boundary

### C. Babbitt Wead Street

- Later name: Lawnview Avenue
- Status: **separate historical street; primary plat needed**
- Location: north of Irving Avenue in Dayton's former Babbitt neighborhood

## Primary-source work still needed

1. Retrieve the deeds from Francis Patterson to John and Robert Wead.
2. Identify John and Robert conclusively within Ebenezer's probate and family record.
3. Retrieve John Wead's 1893 will and the Davis-heirs partition proceeding.
4. Retrieve the 1919 deed or sheriff/partition conveyance for the 53.74-acre remnant to John H. Patterson.
5. Locate the original 1909 court petition and order changing Wead Road to Patterson Road.
6. Locate the original Babbitt plat or Dayton ordinance documenting Wead Street and its change to Lawnview Avenue.
7. Georeference the 1919–1921 Olmsted plans using the railroad and named street intersections.
8. Keep the separate search for Ebenezer's personal farm active rather than substituting the descendants' Oakwood tract.
