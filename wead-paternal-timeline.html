Direct Paternal Wead Timeline

A compact timeline for the public-facing paternal Wead line now represented on the site: John Wead, Ebenezer Wead, James Wead, and John Steele Wead. The emphasis is evidence control—what record anchors each event, and which items remain under review.

  1. c. 1710

    John Wead

    Birth in County Cavan tradition

    The public narrative places John Wead’s origins in County Cavan, Ulster. His later will naming a brother William Wead of Bailieborough is the key transatlantic clue anchoring the Irish connection.

    Local historyNeeds source

  2. 1738

    John Wead

    Rocky Spring land warrant

    John Waid/Wead appears in the Pennsylvania land-warrant evidence for the Rocky Spring settlement, establishing the family’s documented frontier land presence.

    Primary record

  3. 1758–1759

    John Wead

    Provincial Troops service

    John’s 1760 will refers to money due for his service in the Provincial Troops during 1758 and 1759, making his French and Indian War service a direct record-based conclusion.

    Primary record

  4. 1760

    John → Ebenezer

    Will links the land to young Ebenezer

    John’s 1760 will names his plantation near Rocky Spring and provides for his son Ebenezer and daughters. The document matters both for family structure and for the service-pay language.

    Primary record

  5. 1765

    Ebenezer Wead

    Pennsylvania survey returned for Ebenezer

    A 1765 survey records 179 acres and 25 perches for Ebenezer and refers to the earlier improvement made by his father, preserving a father-to-son land connection in the Pennsylvania frontier record.

    Primary record

  6. 1783

    Ebenezer Wead

    Tax-list household evidence

    Ebenezer appears in the 1783 York County tax list as “Ebenezar Wead,” with eleven inhabitants in the household. The Lee’s Legion “Ebenezer Wood” question remains separate and under review.

    Primary recordUnder review

  7. 1789–1790

    John Wead

    Final will, death, and estate inventory

    John’s 1789 will, 1790 death, and estate inventory close the first documented generation on the site, preserving land, kinship, religious-book, and property evidence.

    Primary record

  8. c. 1797–1799

    Ebenezer → James

    Move through Kentucky into the Ohio country

    Later family and church-history evidence supports a Pennsylvania-to-Kentucky-to-Ohio migration pattern, with the Weads entering the Miami Valley before or around Ohio statehood.

    Local historyFamily archive

  9. 1790

    James Wead

    James Wead is born

    James belongs to the generation raised between the old Pennsylvania settlement and the new Greene County, Ohio frontier. His later records anchor him in the Massie’s Creek / Sugar Creek / Xenia Presbyterian world.

    Local historyFamily archive

  10. 26 Oct 1812

    James Wead

    Marriage to Elizabeth Winter

    The marriage register records James Wade and Elizabeth Winter marrying on 26 October 1812 by Rev. Joshua Cannan, a direct record for the couple and a useful spelling-variant anchor.

    Primary record

  11. 1813–1818

    James Wead

    War of 1812 service and Illinois bounty land

    The current reconstruction reads James Wead/Wade as having Ohio militia service and later federal service tied to a 320-acre Illinois Military Tract patent. The Wade/Wead identity link is strong but should still be kept evidence-aware.

    Primary recordUnder review

  12. 11 Jun 1835

    John Steele Wead

    Born near Xenia, Greene County, Ohio

    John Steele’s obituary gives the exact birth date near Xenia, and his discharge paper independently places his birthplace in Greene County, Ohio.

    Primary recordLocal history

  13. 15 Oct 1861–12 Jun 1862

    John Steele Wead

    Company G, 66th Illinois Infantry

    John Steele’s discharge paper records enrollment in Company G and discharge near Corinth, Mississippi, for general disability caused by exposure while performing duty as a soldier.

    Primary record

  14. 24 Dec 1863

    John Steele Wead

    Marriage to Phoebe Butler

    The Coles County, Illinois marriage register records J. S. Wead and Phoebe Butler marrying on Christmas Eve 1863, anchoring John Steele’s Illinois family phase.

    Primary record

  15. 31 May 1866

    James → John Steele

    Father-son letter survives

    James Wead’s handwritten letter to John Steele Wead is a rare family-archive bridge between Greene County, Ohio and the Illinois Civil War generation.

    Family archive

  16. 1872–1873

    James Wead

    Will, death, and Stevenson Cemetery burial

    James’s 1872 will names the family branches and sets up the post-death land question. He died in 1873 and is buried in the old Massie’s Creek graveyard, now Stevenson Cemetery.

    Primary recordFamily archive

  17. 1904

    John Steele Wead

    Death at Paris, Illinois

    John Steele’s obituary places him at 502 North Central Avenue in Paris, Illinois, and preserves later-life family, Methodist, and wartime-memory details that supplement the primary records.
